What this planner does

This planner turns an exam date into a clear daily task. It does not guess your exam date for you. You enter the date from your official date sheet or admission notice. It spreads subjects across the days you have, gives weak subjects more turns, and puts a full revision day every seventh day. If you fall behind, you can make a new plan from today, and the planner will spread the remaining work again.

How to use it

  1. Write your exam name and exam date.
  2. Write how many hours you can really study per day, not the hours you wish you could study.
  3. Write your subjects, one per line. Put a star after any subject you find hard.
  4. Click Make my plan.
  5. Open the Today section every day, tick tasks as you finish them, and use the timer for each study block.
  6. Add flashcards for facts you keep forgetting, and mark them Got it or Still learning.

Worked example

Example 1, a 60 day board plan. A student has 5 subjects and 3 hours per day. That is about 180 hours in total. The planner gives each subject regular turns, gives weak subjects extra turns, and keeps every seventh day for full revision. The student studies a little every day instead of cramming everything in the last week.

Example 2, a 30 day job test plan. A student has 4 topics and 2 hours per day. That is about 60 hours in total. The planner gives each topic a short daily block, with a timed practice set at the end of each week, so speed and accuracy both improve.

Example 3, a 28 day writing gap plan. A student needs to improve one weak skill. The planner gives that skill a block every day, and gives the other skills a shorter block, so the weak skill gets the most practice without ignoring the rest.

A simple weekly rhythm

Study six days, then revise on the seventh day. On revision day, do not start a new chapter. Go back over your flashcards, your ticked tasks, and any mistakes you made that week. This spaced return is what makes revision stick.

Limits, said honestly

This planner is not your board, your university, or your exam body. It does not know your official date sheet, your syllabus, or your merit list. Always check your official date sheet and official notice for dates and rules. This planner does not collect your name, your roll number, or any personal details, and it does not send your plan to a server. If you clear your browser data or change your device, your plan will be gone, so print it or copy it as text first.

Questions

Do I need an account? No. The planner works without an account.

Where is my plan saved? Only in your own browser, on your own device.

What if my exam date changes? Change the date and click Make my plan again. The planner will make a new plan from today.

What if I miss a day? Do not try to do two days of work in one day. Click Make a new plan if I fall behind, and the planner will spread the remaining work again.
